Q: Why does the application store kWh as an integer of hundredths instead of a floating-point double?
Binary floating-point (double) cannot accurately represent base-10 decimal fractions like 0.10 kWh. Using an immutable int hundredths (e.g. 1,234.50 kWh = 123450) matching Postgres numeric(12,2) guarantees zero rounding drift. Subtraction (current - previous) is the ONLY arithmetic allowed on Kwh.

Oral Defense Script Function 1: Why Dial Capture Does Not Compute Money
Instructor / Panelist: "Why did you not calculate the bill amount right inside the mobile app as soon as the meter reader entered the dial reading?"
Mary Tiffany I. Busalanan (Defense Response):
"In rural electric cooperatives like BOHECO I, the tariff rate is not static — it contains generation charges, transmission wheeling rates, universal charges, and lifeline subsidies that fluctuate monthly and are finalized by BOHECO about a week after readings close. If our app computed money on the spot, it would calculate incorrect amounts and mislead consumers.

Therefore, our Clean Architecture deliberately treats electricity billing as a 2-stage decoupled process: First, my Meter Reader role records raw physical energy in Kwh (hundredths of a kWh), creating an unpriced bill in Postgres. Second, 7 days later when BOHECO sends the official billing sheet, Area President Charles transcribes and posts the true whole-peso bill amount via fn_post_bill_amount."
