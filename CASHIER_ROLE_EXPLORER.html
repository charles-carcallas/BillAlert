Why Atomic Multi-Bill Settlement Matters (Defense Talking Point)
When a consumer pays for multiple months (e.g., June, July, August) at once, BillAlert never loops through payments one by one. Doing so would risk leaving a consumer half-paid if the internet cut out mid-loop. Instead, RecordCashPayment passes parallel arrays (p_bill_ids[], p_amounts[]) into a single PostgreSQL stored procedure (fn_record_payment) that executes in one atomic database transaction.

Under BOHECO rural cooperative operating rules, the Meter Reader records kilowatt-hour consumption, but the cooperative billing system in the central office calculates the official monetary tariff. Until Charles (Admin) posts this cooperative figure into BillAlert via fn_post_bill_amount, the bill has no legal peso amount. Cristell cannot collect payment on an estimate because BillAlert never computes tariffs on-device. Both the domain layer and Postgres schema enforce this invariant.

Why Can't the Phone Mint Receipt Numbers Offline? (Defense Talking Point)
Official Receipt numbers are legal financial instruments. If mobile devices generated receipt numbers independently while offline, two cashiers collecting concurrently in different puroks would inevitably mint duplicate receipt numbers (collision). Therefore, in BillAlert, the receipt number is strictly minted by the Supabase PostgreSQL sequence. If a payment is recorded while offline, the app marks it as "Payment confirmation is pending. Check Receipts; do not collect again."
